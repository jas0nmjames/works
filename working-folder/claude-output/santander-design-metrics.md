# Openbank US, launch → today: the design value story

*Working note for portfolio narrative. The spine of this doc is the Openbank US trajectory from the October 2024 national launch to mid-2026, because that's the window where your contribution (including Verizon) is most concrete. Group framing sits around it.*

*Sources: Q2/H1 2026 group financial report, H1 2026 Openbank report, 2025 Annual Report (in `working folder/`), and Santander/Verizon press releases Oct-2024 → Oct-2025. Full source list at the end.*

---

## The headline

**From a standing start in October 2024, Openbank US went to ~$11.4bn in deposits and ~235k customers by June 2026 — while its headline savings rate *fell* from 5.00% to 3.80% APY.** Deposits didn't grow because the rate got more attractive; they grew as the rate got *less* attractive. That points the credit away from "we bought deposits with yield" and toward the things design and partnerships own: a frictionless product, trust, and distribution (Verizon).

That single observation — growth up while rate down — is the strongest analytical hook you have. Lead with it.

## 1. How Santander grades itself (so you attach to the right metric)

Three action lines run through every report:

- **Think Value** — profit, RoTE, EPS, efficiency, capital, payout.
- **Think Customer** — total / active / digital customers, **NPS**, share of products available digitally, cost per active customer.
- **Think Global** — build once, deploy across markets; the bank says its platforms improve *time-to-market, cost-to-serve and customer satisfaction*.

All under **ONE Transformation** ("a digital bank with branches"). The useful part for you: the bank itself draws the causal line from journey/experience work to lower cost-to-serve and higher sales conversion. You attach your work to *that* chain, and to the one metric it audits for experience quality — **NPS** (ranked by country, audited by Stiga/Deloitte).

## 3. What actually improved (answering your list)

**Deposits — up from $0 to ~$11.4bn.** $2bn (Feb'25) → $3bn (Mar'25) → $6bn (Oct'25) → $11.4bn (Jun'26). Roughly doubled in the eight months after the first anniversary, as CDs/new products and the Verizon channel compounded. Improvement is unambiguous and large. *Design linkage:* moderate-to-strong via the <4-min onboarding and "usability/speed/reliability" refinements — an account that's easy and trustworthy to open is a precondition for deposit velocity.

**Customers — 0 → 100k (6 months) → ~235k (Jun'26).** Beat internal expectations at the 100k mark. *Design linkage:* same as deposits; onboarding conversion is the front door.

**New features shipped — savings → a fuller product set.** Launched on high-yield savings; added **CDs**, moved toward **checking / daily-use transaction products** and **payments** through 2025–26; opened a physical **flagship** (Miami Worldcenter) as a digital-first showcase. Every new product is a new design surface. *This is your clearest "I shipped X" material — map your work to specific releases.*

**Partnerships — Verizon is the marquee.** See Section 4. Multi-year, national, embeds a bank account into MyVerizon for millions of subscribers; >$1.3bn of the deposit base traces to it since Apr-25.

**NPS — US rose from 6th to 3rd (2024 → 2025).** A +3-place move — tied with Brazil (5th→2nd) for the largest gain in Santander's audited country ranking that year — against a peer set of Chase, Capital One, BofA, Wells Fargo, PNC, Citi, TD, Truist, M&T, KeyBank, Citizens. This is your strongest *experience-quality* proof point because it's governed and peer-benchmarked. *Design linkage:* this is the metric to claim contribution to.

**Complaints — be careful here; this one did *not* cleanly improve.** In the 2025 Annual Report, Santander **US** formal complaints rose **6,256 → 9,102** (+45%). Caveats that matter: (a) it's *all* Santander US, not Openbank-specific; (b) the base grew enormously; (c) the report attributes much sector complaint growth to Complaints Management Companies / influencers, "significant majority not upheld." So per-customer it may well have improved, but you **can't show that from public data**, and absolute complaints rose. Don't build the story on complaints — build it on **NPS (6→3)**, which moved the right way and is what the bank uses for satisfaction. If you have internal Openbank-only complaint or CSAT data, that's a fill-in-the-blank (Section 6).

## 4. Verizon deep-dive (your meaningful role)

**What it is.** Announced March 18, 2025; a multi-year US partnership putting *Verizon + Openbank Savings* inside Verizon's channels. Qualified Verizon customers earn up to **$180/yr off their Verizon bill** at a $1,000+ average daily balance, on top of a rate ~10× the national average. Sign-up flows through verizon.com / MyVerizon, then hands off to Openbank to complete opening and manage the account.

**Why it matters strategically.** It's distribution at telco scale — a bank account embedded in a carrier's app for millions of users. Ana Botín called it "a significant milestone as we scale our U.S. business." By Jun'26 it accounts for **>$1.3bn** of Openbank US deposits.

**Where design lives in it (your fill-in).** A cross-company embedded flow — Verizon surface → Openbank onboarding → account management — is a hard UX problem: two brands, a handoff, identity/eligibility, and a rewards mechanic tied to daily balance. If you owned or shaped any of: the verizon.com→Openbank handoff, the eligibility/onboarding journey, the rewards-status UI, or the cross-brand design language, that's a flagship case study. The public record gives you the outcome ($1.3bn, national scale); you supply the design decisions behind the handoff.

## 5. The design-attribution framework (which metrics you can claim, and how hard)

Design rarely gets sole credit; rate, macro, and commercial move the same numbers. Tier your claims:

**Tier 1 — lead with these (bank credits experience directly):**

- US NPS 6→3 (audited satisfaction).
- "Usability, transparency, speed, reliability" refinements (their words for the US digital bank).
- <4-minute onboarding as a conversion lever.
- Specific feature launches you shipped (CDs, checking, payments, flagship).

**Tier 2 — claim a share (design contributes with others):**

- Deposits and customer growth (onboarding is the front door, but rate/Verizon/brand also drive them).
- Cost per active customer (Openbank −8% YoY to €214) via self-service and simplification.
- Efficiency ratio (41.6%, −2.9pp) via lower cost-to-serve.

**Tier 3 — context only, don't claim:**

- Openbank underlying profit, RoTE, NII. Cite as the destination of the chain, attributed to the business, not to design.

**The causal chain to reuse:** frictionless onboarding + trusted experience + Verizon distribution → deposits and customers up *despite a falling rate* → NPS 6→3 → lower cost-to-serve. Design owns the front of that chain.

## 7. Talking about it credibly

- **Lead with the falling-rate hook and NPS 6→3.** Both are true, sourced, and flattering without overclaiming.
- **Attach to the bank's own causal language** ("Santander credits usability/speed/reliability improvements… I owned [X]") rather than asserting you drove deposits.
- **Contribution, not causation.** "Contributed to," "part of the team that," paired with your pre/post evidence. No sole-cause claims on deposits or profit.
- **Pair each public number with a private one** (public NPS rank + your CSAT; public "<4 min" + your usability deltas; public $1.3bn Verizon + your handoff decisions).
- **One metric per story.** A case study lands on a single number moved — NPS 6→3, or Verizon $1.3bn, or reprints avoided — not a dashboard.
- **Handle complaints proactively, quietly.** If asked, note the base grew and CMC-driven volume, and pivot to NPS. Don't feature it.
